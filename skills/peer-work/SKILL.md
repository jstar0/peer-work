---
name: peer-work
description: Work alongside another agent when requested or when authorized collaboration would help. Either of you can frame the question, try an approach, or change the direction of the work. Also use for handoffs across models or sessions.
---
When the user asks to use peer-work, bring a peer in while they can still change the work. Use the requested model and channel. If it's unavailable, say so. When the choice is yours, weigh what a peer could contribute in craft, time saved, cost, or independent judgment against the effort of getting them up to speed. Sometimes doing it yourself is quicker.

When you want independent views, the user's own request can be the peer's entire first turn. Give them the same original material and the decisions the user has already made. Use a fresh conversation if the history or shared notes give away an answer. Form your own view while they read. Once you hear theirs, bring your questions to the part that caught their eye. When you're shaping an existing draft together, share the draft.

A playable scene can show why a user kept it. An image-generated mockup can let you try a different look before replacing it. If a change would displace something the user chose, keep the original available and show the alternative.

The agent talking to the user owns the outcome. When the direction for the next piece of work is settled, carry it through and check the result. While the direction is still taking shape, bring the user your best current idea while they can still steer it. Ask if their preference would settle a choice. Use an existing example or a small sketch if seeing the difference would help. When a peer makes a case for a different direction, put their reason beside your recommendation in the reply where you make that choice.

Either of you can lead the next attempt. You can follow a peer's promising idea even while you have doubts. Pick one you have reason to believe in and try it on the user's actual problem. If it rests on how people work, look at something they made. Other possibilities can wait. If the idea falls apart, figuring out what to try next is still part of your work. A Codex Composer Prediction can suggest a next move within the user's request.

When you pass an idea along, say whose it was. “Claude suggested this” leaves the next peer free to disagree. For work spanning sessions or context windows, leave the current request and its limits where the next agent will actually pick up the work. On return, read that alongside the latest result: the last visible message may belong to a question already answered. If someone reacted to an earlier version, include the version they saw. Resume the work from there.

See [Client notes](references/local-clients.md) when choosing a model or resuming a peer conversation.
