---
name: peer-work
description: Work alongside another agent when requested or when authorized collaboration would help. Either of you can frame the question, try an approach, or change the direction of the work. Also use for handoffs across models or sessions.
---
When the user asks to use peer-work, bring a peer in while they can still change the work. Use the requested model and channel. If it's unavailable, say so. When the choice is yours, weigh what a peer could contribute in craft, time saved, cost, or independent judgment against the effort of getting them up to speed. Sometimes doing it yourself is quicker.

When you want independent views, the user's own words can be the whole brief. Give the peer access to the same original work and the decisions the user has already made. Use a fresh conversation if the history or shared notes give away an answer. Form your own view while they read and save it for the exchange. When you're shaping an existing draft together, share the draft.

A playable scene can show why a user kept it. An image-generated mockup can let you try a different look before replacing it. If a change would displace something the user chose, keep the original available and show the alternative.

Sometimes a peer sends you a draft addressed to the user. If it puts words in the user's mouth or hands them unfinished research, answer the peer. Bring what the user actually said, what you found in the work, or how you'd proceed with a fact still unknown. Give them a chance to change your mind. You can keep a good idea even when its author misread the request. Credit the idea and say what convinced you.

The agent talking to the user owns the outcome. Answer what they came to ask. If a fact is missing, look for it in the original work. If your recommendation holds either way, give it and keep the question for the work that needs it. When the direction turns on the user's taste or firsthand knowledge, bring your best idea while they can still steer and show what their answer would change. An existing example or a small sketch can help. If a peer has a reason that could change the choice, put it alongside yours in that reply.

Either of you can lead the next attempt. You can follow a peer's promising idea even while you have doubts. Pick one you have reason to believe in and try it on the user's actual problem. If it rests on how people work, look at something they made. Carry a settled direction through and check the result. If the idea falls apart, figuring out what to try next is still part of your work. A Codex Composer Prediction can suggest a next move within the user's request.

For work spanning sessions or context windows, leave the current request and its limits where the next agent will actually pick up the work. On return, read that alongside the latest result. The last visible message may belong to a question already answered. If someone reacted to an earlier version, include the version they saw. Resume the work from there.

See [Client notes](references/local-clients.md) when choosing a model or resuming a peer conversation.
